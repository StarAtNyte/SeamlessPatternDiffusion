Abstract
Abstract patterns feature non-representational designs that don't depict recognizable objects or forms. These patterns emphasize color, shape, and composition over realistic imagery. They often include irregular shapes, bold color blocks, asymmetrical arrangements, and artistic interpretations that can range from subtle and sophisticated to bold and expressive. Abstract patterns are popular in contemporary and modern interior design.

Geometric
Geometric patterns are based on mathematical shapes and forms like circles, squares, triangles, diamonds, hexagons, and lines. These designs often feature repeating motifs, symmetrical arrangements, and precise angular forms. Common examples include Greek key patterns, chevron designs, lattice work, and tessellations. Geometric patterns can be simple or complex and work well in both traditional and modern settings.

Damask
Damask is a classic weaving pattern that creates reversible designs through contrasting lustrous and matte finishes. Originally from Damascus, these patterns typically feature elaborate floral motifs, scrollwork, and ornate designs woven into the fabric structure itself. The pattern appears in relief against the background, creating subtle tonal variations. Damask patterns are often formal and elegant, commonly used in luxury textiles.

Floral
Floral patterns incorporate botanical elements like flowers, leaves, vines, and plants. These can range from realistic botanical illustrations to stylized interpretations of natural forms. Floral designs might feature single large blooms, scattered small flowers, trailing vines, or complete garden scenes. They're among the most popular and timeless pattern categories, suitable for both traditional and contemporary applications.

Border
Border patterns are decorative bands or frames that run along the edges or perimeters of textiles and rugs. These patterns often complement the main design and can include geometric motifs, floral elements, or cultural symbols. Borders help define the textile's boundaries and can range from simple single lines to elaborate multi-layered designs with intricate details.

Mandala
Mandala patterns feature circular, symmetrical designs radiating from a central point. Originating from spiritual and cultural traditions, these patterns represent wholeness and cosmic order. They typically include concentric circles with geometric or floral elements arranged in perfect symmetry. Mandala designs can be simple or highly complex with multiple layers of detail expanding outward from the center.

Mirror
Mirror patterns (also called mirrored or reflected patterns) create symmetrical designs where one half of the pattern is an exact reflection of the other half. This can occur along vertical, horizontal, or diagonal axes. The effect creates balanced, harmonious designs that are pleasing to the eye. Mirror patterns can incorporate any other design elements but arranged in perfect symmetrical reflection.

Minimalistic / Modern
Minimalistic patterns emphasize simplicity, clean lines, and uncluttered designs. These patterns often feature subtle textures, monochromatic color schemes, simple geometric forms, or sparse decorative elements with plenty of negative space. The focus is on quality over quantity, with each design element serving a purpose. These patterns complement contemporary architecture and modern interior design aesthetics.

Organic
Organic patterns are inspired by natural forms and flowing, irregular shapes found in nature. Unlike geometric patterns, organic designs feature curved lines, asymmetrical compositions, and forms that mimic natural growth patterns, water flow, or cellular structures. These patterns often have a soft, flowing quality and can include abstract interpretations of natural phenomena like waves, clouds, or biological forms.

Rug (Traditional)
Traditional rug patterns refer to classic designs that have been passed down through generations within specific cultural and regional weaving traditions. This includes Persian medallion patterns, Turkish kilim designs, Caucasian geometric motifs, Indian paisley patterns, and Moroccan tribal designs. These patterns often carry cultural significance and are characterized by specific color palettes, symbolic motifs, and time-honored weaving techniques that define their regional origins.
